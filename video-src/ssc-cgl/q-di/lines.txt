# SSC CGL Quant — Data interpretation
Welcome! This SSC CGL lesson is about data interpretation. || Welcome! This S S C C G L lesson is about data interpretation: reading tables, bar charts, pie charts and line graphs.
Read the title and the units before you calculate. || Before you calculate, read the title, the units and what each axis means. Most mistakes come from using the wrong base or the wrong unit.
% change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100. || Most questions ask for percentage change. Sales rose from one hundred and twenty to one hundred and fifty. That is thirty on one hundred and twenty, or twenty five percent.
The biggest jump is not always the biggest percentage. || The biggest jump in value is not always the biggest percentage. A rise of forty five from one hundred and thirty five is thirty three percent, more than a rise of thirty six from one hundred and eighty.
Pie charts: 1% = 3.6°. || In a pie chart, the whole circle is three hundred and sixty degrees, so one percent is three point six degrees. Twenty five percent is ninety degrees. A fifty four degree sector is fifteen percent of the total.
“What % of” divides by the second; “% more than” divides the difference. || Watch the wording. A is what percent of B divides A by B. A is what percent more than B divides the difference by B. Seven fifty out of two thousand is thirty seven and a half percent.
Approximate when the options are far apart. || When the options are far apart, round the numbers and estimate. To compare two fractions, cross multiply instead of dividing.
Try this: 120 to 150. The answer is A, 25%. || Try this. Sales rose from one hundred and twenty to one hundred and fifty. What is the percentage increase? The answer is A, twenty five percent.
