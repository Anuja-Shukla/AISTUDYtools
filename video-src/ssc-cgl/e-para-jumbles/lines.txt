# SSC CGL English — Sentence rearrangement
Welcome! This SSC CGL lesson is about sentence rearrangement. || Welcome! This S S C C G L English lesson is about sentence rearrangement, also called para jumbles.
Step one: find the opener. || Step one is to find the opening sentence. It introduces the topic and usually names a person or thing in full. It rarely starts with he, she, they, so or however.
Step two: follow the pronouns. || Step two is to follow the links. Pronouns point back. They raised money needs a group mentioned before it, such as a group of students decided to help.
Connectors show the order. || Connectors show the order too. So and therefore show a result. However and but show a contrast. Also adds a similar point.
Time words make a sequence. || Time words make a sequence: first, then, next and finally. At first, followed by within a month, shows change over time.
Find a pair that must go together. || Look for two sentences that must sit together. This process is called photosynthesis must come right after the sentence that describes the process.
Use the options to eliminate. || Once you know the opener, cross out options that start differently. Then check one pair you are sure of. Usually only one option is left.
Try this in the practice set. || Now try the practice set. Each paragraph has one clear order, built from pronouns, connectors and time words.
