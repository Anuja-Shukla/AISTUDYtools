# SSC CGL Reasoning — Emotional and social intelligence
Welcome! This SSC CGL lesson is about emotional and social intelligence. || Welcome! This S S C C G L lesson is about emotional and social intelligence, which appears in Tier 2.
Understand and manage emotions — yours and others'. || Emotional intelligence is the ability to understand and manage your own emotions, and to understand and work well with other people.
Five components. || A widely used model, popularised by Daniel Goleman, has five parts: self awareness, self regulation, motivation, empathy and social skills.
Match each definition to its part. || Self awareness is knowing your own emotions. Self regulation is controlling impulses and moods. Motivation is an inner drive to achieve. Empathy is understanding others' feelings. Social skills means managing relationships.
Best response: calm, honest, respectful, solves the problem. || In situation questions, the best response is usually calm, honest and respectful, and it solves the problem. Where possible, it happens in private.
Assertive beats aggressive and passive. || Prefer the assertive style, which states needs clearly and respectfully. Reject aggressive answers that attack, passive answers that give in, and passive aggressive answers with hidden anger.
Worked situation: a colleague takes credit for your idea. || If a colleague takes credit for your idea, do not shout in the meeting and do not stay silent forever. Speak to them privately and calmly clarify your part.
Try this: a customer is angry about a delay. The answer is A. || Try this. A customer is angry about a delay. The best answer is A: stay calm, apologise, explain the reason and offer a solution.
Recap. Now try the tools and the practice questions below. || To recap. Learn the five components. Choose calm, honest, problem solving responses. And prefer the assertive style. Now try the tools and the practice questions below this video.
