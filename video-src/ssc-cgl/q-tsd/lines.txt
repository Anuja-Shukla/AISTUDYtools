# SSC CGL Quant — Time, speed & distance
Welcome! This SSC CGL lesson is about time, speed and distance. || Welcome! This S S C C G L lesson is about time, speed and distance, including trains, boats and races.
Distance = speed × time. Convert km/h to m/s with 5/18. || Distance equals speed times time. To change kilometres per hour into metres per second, multiply by five eighteenths. Seventy two kilometres per hour is twenty metres per second.
Relative speed: add when moving towards each other, subtract when chasing. || When two objects move towards each other, add their speeds. When one chases the other, subtract. Two cars three hundred kilometres apart, at forty and sixty, meet in three hours.
A train covers its own length plus whatever it crosses. || A train must cover its own length, plus the length of whatever it crosses. A hundred and fifty metre train at fifteen metres per second passes a pole in ten seconds, and a two hundred and fifty metre platform in twenty six and two thirds seconds.
Two trains: add the lengths, use the relative speed. || For two trains, add their lengths and use the relative speed. Opposite directions add speeds. Same direction subtracts them.
Boats: downstream b + s, upstream b − s. || In boats and streams, downstream speed is boat plus stream, and upstream is boat minus stream. So the boat's speed is the average of the two, and the stream is half the difference.
Races: A beats B by 20 m in 100 m means speeds 5 : 4. || In races, if A beats B by twenty metres in a hundred metre race, then while A runs a hundred, B runs eighty. Their speeds are in the ratio five to four.
Try this: thief 2 km ahead at 10 km/h, police at 12. The answer is A, 1 hour. || Try this. A thief is two kilometres ahead, running at ten kilometres per hour. A policeman follows at twelve. When does he catch the thief? The answer is A, in one hour. The gap closes at two kilometres per hour.
