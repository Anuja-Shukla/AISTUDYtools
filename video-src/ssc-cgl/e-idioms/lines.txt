# SSC CGL English — Idioms and phrases
Welcome! This SSC CGL lesson is about idioms and phrases. || Welcome! This S S C C G L English lesson is about idioms and phrases.
An idiom means more than its words. || An idiom is a group of words whose meaning is different from the separate words. It is raining cats and dogs means it is raining heavily. The literal option is almost always a trap.
Secrets and talk. || Learn idioms by theme. For secrets and talk: spill the beans and let the cat out of the bag mean reveal a secret. Beat around the bush means avoid the main point. Break the ice means start talking.
Time and effort. || For time and effort: once in a blue moon means very rarely. At the eleventh hour means at the last moment. Burn the midnight oil means work late. A piece of cake means very easy.
Trouble and feelings. || For trouble and feelings: in hot water means in trouble. Under the weather means slightly ill. Bite the bullet means face something hard bravely. Bury the hatchet means make peace.
Money and value. || For money: cost an arm and a leg means very expensive. A white elephant is costly but useless. A blessing in disguise is something good that first looked bad.
Picture it, and read the tone. || If you are unsure, picture the idiom and read the sentence around it. Is the tone positive or negative? That often rules out two options.
Try this: once in a blue moon. The answer is A, very rarely. || Try this. What does once in a blue moon mean? The answer is A, very rarely.
