# SSC CGL Quant — Mixtures & alligation
Welcome! This SSC CGL lesson is about mixtures and alligation. || Welcome! This S S C C G L lesson is about mixtures and alligation, a shortcut you will use again and again.
When two things are mixed, the mixture's value lies between them. || When two things with different values are mixed, the value of the mixture lies between them. Alligation tells you the mixing ratio in a single step.
Cheaper : dearer = (dearer − mean) : (mean − cheaper). || Draw a cross. Rice at forty and sixty rupees is mixed to cost forty six. The cheaper rice gets sixty minus forty six, fourteen. The dearer rice gets forty six minus forty, six. So the ratio is fourteen to six, or seven to three.
The same rule works for percentages and averages. || The same rule works for strengths. Twenty percent and fifty percent acid mixed to get thirty percent: fifty minus thirty is twenty, thirty minus twenty is ten, so two to one.
Water costs nothing. || Treat water as costing zero. Milk at fifty rupees a litre, brought down to forty: water gets fifty minus forty, ten, and milk gets forty minus zero, forty. Water to milk is one to four.
Removal and replacement: multiply by (1 − y/x) each time. || When y litres are taken out of x litres and replaced with water, n times, the milk left is x times one minus y over x, to the power n. Forty litres, four litres replaced twice, leaves forty times zero point nine times zero point nine, thirty two point four litres.
Mixing two mixtures: average the fractions. || To mix equal amounts of two mixtures, average the fractions. Three to one is three quarters milk. Five to three is five eighths milk. Their average is eleven sixteenths, so the ratio is eleven to five.
Try this: tea at 30 and 45 mixed to 40. The answer is A, 1 : 2. || Try this. Tea at thirty and forty five rupees is mixed to get forty. In what ratio? The answer is A, one to two. Forty five minus forty is five, forty minus thirty is ten.
