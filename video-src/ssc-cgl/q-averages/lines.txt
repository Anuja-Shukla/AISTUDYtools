# SSC CGL Quant — Averages
Welcome! This SSC CGL lesson is about averages. || Welcome! This S S C C G L lesson is about averages, a topic that appears in almost every paper.
Average = sum ÷ count. Work with totals, not averages. || The average is the sum divided by the number of items. So the sum is the average times the count. The secret to most average questions is to work with totals, not averages.
Deviation method: pick a base, add the average of the differences. || For a long list of close numbers, use the deviation method. Pick a base, like seventy five. The differences from it add up to five, so the average is seventy five plus one, which is seventy six.
Someone joins: new total minus old total. || When someone joins, subtract the old total from the new total. Ten students average fifteen. With the teacher, eleven people average sixteen. One hundred and seventy six minus one hundred and fifty gives the teacher's age, twenty six.
Replacement: new item = old item + count × change. || When one person replaces another, the count stays the same. The new person equals the old person, plus the count times the change in average. Eight people, average up by one and a half, replacing fifty two kilograms, gives sixty four.
Combined average: add the totals, divide by the total count. || To combine two groups, add their totals and divide by the total count. Thirty students averaging sixty, and twenty averaging seventy, combine to sixty four. The bigger group pulls the average towards itself.
Average speed over equal distances is 2xy ÷ (x + y), not the simple mean. || Here is the classic trap. If you go at forty and return at sixty, the average speed is not fifty. It is two times forty times sixty, divided by one hundred, which is forty eight. You spend longer at the slower speed.
Try this: 11th innings, score 90, average up by 5. The answer is A, 40. || Try this. A batsman scores ninety in his eleventh innings, and his average rises by five. What is his new average? The answer is A, forty. Ninety minus ten times five is forty.
Recap. Now try the tools and the practice questions below. || To recap. Total equals average times count. For joining, leaving and replacing, compare totals. For combined groups, add the totals.
