# SSC CGL Quant — Number system
Welcome! This SSC CGL lesson is about the number system, the base of all Quant. || Welcome! This S S C C G L lesson is about the number system, the base of all of quantitative aptitude.
Primes have exactly two factors. 1 is neither prime nor composite; 2 is the only even prime. || A prime number has exactly two factors, like two, three, five and seven. A composite number has more than two. Two numbers whose H C F is one are called co-prime. Remember, one is neither prime nor composite, and two is the only even prime.
Divisibility rules save you from long division. || Divisibility rules save you from long division. For three or nine, add the digits. For four or eight, look at the last two or three digits. For six, check two and three. For eleven, subtract the sum of digits in even places from the sum in odd places; the answer must be zero or a multiple of eleven.
Number of factors = (p+1)(q+1)(r+1). 360 has 24 factors. || To count factors, first write the number as powers of primes. Then add one to each power and multiply. Three hundred and sixty is two cubed, times three squared, times five. So it has four times three times two, which is twenty four factors.
HCF takes the lowest powers, LCM the highest. HCF × LCM = product. || For H C F, take the lowest power of each common prime. For L C M, take the highest power of every prime. For twelve and eighteen, the H C F is six and the L C M is thirty six. And for two numbers, H C F times L C M equals their product.
Least or together means LCM; greatest means HCF. || Read the key word. Least, or together again, means L C M. Greatest or largest means H C F. If every division leaves the same remainder, add that remainder to the L C M.
Unit digits repeat in cycles of 4. 7 to the power 35 ends in 3. || Unit digits of powers repeat in cycles of at most four. For seven, the cycle is seven, nine, three, one. To find the unit digit of seven to the power thirty five, divide thirty five by four. The remainder is three, so the unit digit is three.
Try this: bells at 12, 15 and 18 minutes. The answer is A, 3 hours. || Try this. Three bells ring every twelve, fifteen and eighteen minutes. When do they ring together again? The answer is A, three hours, because the L C M of twelve, fifteen and eighteen is one hundred and eighty minutes.
