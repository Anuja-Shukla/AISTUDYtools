# SSC CGL Quant — Simple & compound interest
Welcome! This SSC CGL lesson is about simple and compound interest. || Welcome! This S S C C G L lesson is about simple and compound interest.
Simple interest = P × R × T ÷ 100. The interest is the same every year. || Simple interest is P times R times T, divided by a hundred. Five thousand rupees at eight percent for three years earns twelve hundred rupees. With simple interest, the interest is the same every year.
Doubling under SI: R × T = 100. || A sum doubles when the interest equals the principal. So R times T equals a hundred. If a sum doubles in eight years, the rate is twelve and a half percent. For n times, R times T is a hundred times n minus one.
Compound interest: A = P(1 + R/100)ⁿ. Interest earns interest. || With compound interest, each year's interest is added to the principal. The amount is P times one plus R over a hundred, to the power n. Ten thousand at ten percent for two years becomes twelve thousand one hundred.
CI is successive percentage change: 2 years at 10% is 21%. || Compound interest is just successive percentage change. Two years at ten percent is ten plus ten plus one, which is twenty one percent. Three years at ten percent is thirty three point one percent.
CI − SI for 2 years = P(R/100)². || The difference between compound and simple interest for two years is P times R over a hundred, squared. On ten thousand at ten percent, that is one hundred rupees. For three years, multiply by three plus R over a hundred, giving three hundred and ten.
Half-yearly: halve the rate, double the periods. || When interest is compounded half-yearly, halve the rate and double the number of periods. For quarterly, divide the rate by four and multiply the periods by four.

What is the sum? The answer is A, ten thousand rupees, because twenty five divided by zero point zero zero two five is ten thousand.
