Each extra samosa gives Isha less satisfaction: 30, 24, 18, 12, then 6 rupees' worth.
At 15 rupees each, she buys while marginal utility is at least the price: three samosas. || At fifteen rupees each, she buys while marginal utility is at least the price. That is three samosas.
She would pay 72 but pays 45: her consumer surplus is 27. || She would be willing to pay seventy two rupees, but pays forty five. Her consumer surplus is twenty seven.
With several goods, the consumer is in equilibrium when marginal utility per rupee is equal across them.
Indifference curves show equal satisfaction, slope down, are convex, never cross, and higher is better. Equilibrium is where MRS equals the price ratio.
