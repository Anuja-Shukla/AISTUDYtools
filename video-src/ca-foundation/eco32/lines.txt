# Business Economics Lesson 3.2 — The Keynesian Theory of Income
Welcome! This lesson is about the Keynesian theory of income determination.
Keynes argued that in the short run, aggregate demand, made of consumption, investment, government spending and net exports, sets output.
Consumption is autonomous spending plus the marginal propensity to consume times income. MPC plus MPS equals one.
With consumption 200 plus 0.8 Y and investment 300, equilibrium income is 2,500 crore. || With consumption of two hundred plus zero point eight Y, and investment of three hundred, equilibrium income is two thousand five hundred crore.
The multiplier is one over MPS: 5. An extra 100 of investment raises income by 500. || The multiplier is one over the marginal propensity to save, which is five. An extra hundred of investment raises income by five hundred.
Too little demand gives a deflationary gap and unemployment; too much gives an inflationary gap.
