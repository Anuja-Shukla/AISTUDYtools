# Accounting Lesson 1.1 — Meaning and Scope of Accounting
Welcome! This lesson is about meaning and scope of accounting.
Accounting identifies, records, classifies, summarises, interprets and communicates financial information. Only events measured in money are recorded.
Meera's investment and her credit purchase are recorded. A hard-working employee is not, because it has no money value, and a contract for next month is not a transaction yet. || Meera's investment and her credit purchase are recorded. A hard-working employee is not, because it has no money value. And a contract to buy a shelf next month is not a transaction yet.
Book-keeping is the recording stage. Accounting goes further, summarising and interpreting. Accountancy is the whole body of knowledge behind both.
Internal users run the business: owners and managers. External users judge it from outside: banks, investors, the tax department, suppliers and others.
Accounting keeps a systematic record, finds the result through the profit and loss account, and shows the financial position through the balance sheet.
Accounting has limits: it ignores what money cannot measure, uses historical cost, depends on estimates, and can be window-dressed.
Try this: which item is recorded in the books? Goods sold for cash, because it is a money-measurable transaction of the business. || Try this. Which item is recorded in the books? The answer is A, goods sold for cash, because it is a money-measurable transaction of the business.
