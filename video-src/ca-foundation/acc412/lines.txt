# Accounting Lesson 4.12 — Redemption of Debentures
Welcome! This lesson is about the redemption of debentures.
Debentures can be redeemed in a lump sum, in instalments, by buying them in the open market, or by conversion into shares.
Under the current rules, an unlisted company creates a debenture redemption reserve of 10 percent of debentures outstanding: 60,000 here. || Under the current rules, an unlisted company creates a debenture redemption reserve of ten percent of debentures outstanding. Sixty thousand here.
It must also invest or deposit 15 percent of the amount maturing in the year, by 30 April: 90,000. || It must also invest or deposit fifteen percent of the amount maturing in the year, by the thirtieth of April. Ninety thousand here.
If the company buys back 1 lakh of its own debentures for 96,000 and cancels them, the 4,000 profit goes to capital reserve. || If the company buys back one lakh of its own debentures for ninety six thousand and cancels them, the four thousand profit goes to capital reserve.
Once the debentures are redeemed, the DRR is transferred to general reserve.
