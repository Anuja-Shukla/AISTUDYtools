# Lesson 3.2 — Measures of Central Tendency
Welcome! This lesson is about averages: the mean, the median and the mode, plus the geometric and harmonic means.
An average is one value that represents a whole data set. Which average you use depends on the data and the question.
Here are 30 days of sales at Kiran Traders, grouped into classes: 10–20 units on 4 days, 20–30 on 6, 30–40 on 10, 40–50 on 7 and 50–60 on 3. || Here are thirty days of sales at Kiran Traders, grouped into classes. Ten to twenty units on four days, twenty to thirty on six, thirty to forty on ten, forty to fifty on seven, and fifty to sixty on three.
For grouped data, the mean uses the mid-values: Σfx ÷ Σf. Here Σfx = 1,040, so the mean is 1,040 ÷ 30, about 34.67 units. || For grouped data, the mean uses the mid-values. Multiply each mid-value by its frequency, add them up, and divide by the total frequency. Here the total is one thousand and forty, so the mean is one thousand and forty divided by thirty, about thirty-four point six seven units.
The median is the middle value of ordered data. For 3, 5, 8, 10 and 12, it is 8. For grouped data, we use a formula. || The median is the middle value of ordered data. For three, five, eight, ten and twelve, it is eight. For grouped data, we use a formula.
N/2 = 15. The cumulative frequencies are 4, 10, 20, so the median class is 30–40. Median = 30 + (15 − 10) ÷ 10 × 10 = 35. || N over two is fifteen. The cumulative frequencies are four, ten, twenty, so the median class is thirty to forty. The median is thirty, plus fifteen minus ten, over ten, times ten. That is thirty-five.
The mode is the most frequent value. The modal class is 30–40. Mode = 30 + (10 − 6) ÷ (20 − 6 − 7) × 10, about 35.71. || The mode is the most frequent value. The modal class is thirty to forty. The mode is thirty, plus ten minus six, over twenty minus six minus seven, times ten. That is about thirty-five point seven one.
A useful check, the empirical relation: mode ≈ 3 median − 2 mean. Here 3 × 35 − 2 × 34.67 ≈ 35.67, very close to 35.71. || A useful check is the empirical relation. Mode is about three times the median, minus two times the mean. Here, three times thirty-five, minus two times thirty-four point six seven, is about thirty-five point six seven. Very close to thirty-five point seven one.
Two properties of the mean: deviations from it always add up to zero, and two groups combine as (n₁M₁ + n₂M₂) ÷ (n₁ + n₂), where M is each group's mean. 20 students averaging 60 and 30 averaging 70 give 66. || Two properties of the mean. Deviations from it always add up to zero. And two groups combine as n one times M one, plus n two times M two, over n one plus n two, where M is each group's mean. Twenty students averaging sixty, and thirty averaging seventy, give a combined mean of sixty-six.
The geometric mean suits growth rates. The harmonic mean suits average speeds. At 40 km/h one way and 60 km/h back, the average speed is the HM, 48 km/h, not 50. And AM ≥ GM ≥ HM. || The geometric mean suits growth rates. The harmonic mean suits average speeds. At forty kilometres an hour one way, and sixty back, the average speed is the harmonic mean, forty-eight, not fifty. And the arithmetic mean is always at least the geometric mean, which is at least the harmonic mean.
Choosing an average: the median for skewed data like incomes, the mode for the most common size, and the mean when every value matters.
