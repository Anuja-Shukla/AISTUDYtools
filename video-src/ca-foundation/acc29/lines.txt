# Accounting Lesson 2.9 — Depreciation
Welcome! This lesson is about depreciation: what it is, the two main methods, and what happens when an asset is sold.
Depreciation spreads the cost of a long-lived asset, such as a machine, over the years in which it earns revenue. It is a matching of cost with revenue, not a way of valuing the asset at market price.
To calculate it you need three things: the cost of the asset, its residual value at the end of its life, and its useful life in years. || To calculate it, you need three things. The cost of the asset, its residual value at the end of its life, and its useful life in years.
Sunrise Textiles buys a machine for ₹5,00,000. It expects to sell it for ₹50,000 after five years. || Sunrise Textiles buys a machine for five lakh rupees. It expects to sell it for fifty thousand rupees after five years.
Straight line method: charge the same amount every year. (₹5,00,000 − ₹50,000) ÷ 5 = ₹90,000 a year. || Under the straight line method, you charge the same amount every year. Five lakh, minus fifty thousand, divided by five, gives ninety thousand rupees a year.
Written down value method: charge a fixed rate on the opening book value. At 20%, the charges are ₹1,00,000, then ₹80,000, then ₹64,000, and so on. || Under the written down value method, you charge a fixed rate on the opening book value each year. At twenty percent, the charges are one lakh, then eighty thousand, then sixty four thousand, and so on.
Compare them: the straight line charge stays flat, while the WDV charge is highest in the early years and falls each year. || Compare the two. The straight line charge stays flat. The written down value charge is highest in the early years, and falls every year after that.
Which to use? SLM suits assets used evenly over their life. WDV suits assets that give more benefit early on, and whose repairs grow with age. || Which one should you use? The straight line method suits assets used evenly over their life. The written down value method suits assets that give more benefit early on, and whose repairs grow with age.
At the end of year 2, under SLM, the book value is ₹5,00,000 − 2 × ₹90,000 = ₹3,20,000. If the machine is sold for ₹3,30,000, there is a profit on sale of ₹10,000. || At the end of year two, under the straight line method, the book value is five lakh minus two times ninety thousand, which is three lakh twenty thousand. If the machine is sold for three lakh thirty thousand, there is a profit on sale of ten thousand rupees.
Try this: a van costs ₹4,00,000, with no residual value and a life of 8 years. What is the annual SLM charge? It is ₹4,00,000 ÷ 8 = ₹50,000. || Try this. A van costs four lakh rupees, with no residual value and a life of eight years. What is the annual straight line charge? Four lakh divided by eight is fifty thousand rupees. The answer is A.
Remember: SLM charges (cost − residual) ÷ life every year; WDV charges a fixed rate on the falling book value; profit or loss on sale is the sale price minus book value.
