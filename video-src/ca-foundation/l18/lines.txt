# Lesson 1.8 — Calculus in Business
Welcome! This lesson applies calculus to business: derivatives, marginal cost, maxima and minima, and integration.
The derivative dy/dx measures how fast y changes as x changes. On a graph, it is the slope of the curve at a point. || The derivative, d y by d x, measures how fast y changes as x changes. On a graph, it is the slope of the curve at a point.
The power rule does most of the work: d/dx(xⁿ) = nxⁿ⁻¹. Constants give 0, and sums are differentiated term by term. So d/dx(5x³ + 2x − 7) = 15x² + 2. || The power rule does most of the work. The derivative of x to the n is n x to the n minus one. Constants give zero, and sums are differentiated term by term. So the derivative of five x cubed plus two x minus seven is fifteen x squared plus two.
Also know: d/dx(eˣ) = eˣ and d/dx(log x) = 1/x. For products, quotients and functions inside functions, use the product, quotient and chain rules. || Also know these. The derivative of e to the x is e to the x. The derivative of log x is one over x. And for products, quotients, and functions inside functions, use the product, quotient and chain rules.
If C(x) is total cost, marginal cost MC = dC/dx: roughly the cost of one more unit. For C = 2x² + 20x + 800, MC = 4x + 20. || If C of x is total cost, the marginal cost is d C by d x. It is roughly the cost of making one more unit. For C equals two x squared plus twenty x plus eight hundred, the marginal cost is four x plus twenty.
Average cost is C/x = 2x + 20 + 800/x. Its derivative, 2 − 800/x², is zero at x = 20. There average cost is 100, which equals marginal cost. || Average cost is C over x, which is two x plus twenty plus eight hundred over x. Its derivative, two minus eight hundred over x squared, is zero at x equals twenty. There, the average cost is a hundred, exactly equal to the marginal cost.
To find a maximum or minimum, set f′(x) = 0. Then check the second derivative: f″ < 0 means a maximum, f″ > 0 means a minimum. || To find a maximum or a minimum, set f dash of x equal to zero. Then check the second derivative. If it is negative, you have a maximum. If it is positive, a minimum.
Revenue is R = 200x − x², so profit P = −3x² + 180x − 800. P′ = −6x + 180 = 0 gives x = 30, and P″ = −6 < 0. Maximum profit is 1,900. || Revenue is two hundred x minus x squared, so profit is minus three x squared, plus a hundred and eighty x, minus eight hundred. P dash equals minus six x plus a hundred and eighty, which is zero at x equals thirty. P double dash is minus six, negative, so this is a maximum. The maximum profit is one thousand nine hundred.
Integration reverses differentiation: ∫xⁿ dx = xⁿ⁺¹/(n + 1) + C. Also ∫(1/x) dx = log x + C and ∫eˣ dx = eˣ + C. || Integration reverses differentiation. The integral of x to the n is x to the n plus one, over n plus one, plus a constant C. The integral of one over x is log x, and the integral of e to the x is e to the x.
Given MC = 4x + 20, integrate: C = 2x² + 20x + K. The constant K is the fixed cost, 800, so C(x) = 2x² + 20x + 800. || Given a marginal cost of four x plus twenty, integrate to get two x squared plus twenty x, plus a constant. That constant is the fixed cost, eight hundred. So total cost is two x squared plus twenty x plus eight hundred.
A definite integral gives a total between two limits: F(b) − F(a). For example, ∫₀² 3x² dx = 2³ − 0³ = 8. || A definite integral gives a total between two limits: F of b minus F of a. For example, the integral of three x squared, from zero to two, is two cubed minus zero cubed, which is eight.
Try this: if C(x) = x³ − 6x² + 15x, what is the marginal cost at x = 3? MC = 3x² − 12x + 15 = 27 − 36 + 15 = 6. || Try this. If C of x equals x cubed minus six x squared plus fifteen x, what is the marginal cost at x equals three? The marginal cost is three x squared minus twelve x plus fifteen. At three, that is twenty-seven minus thirty-six plus fifteen, which is six. The answer is A.
