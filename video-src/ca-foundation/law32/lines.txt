# Business Laws Lesson 3.2 — Conditions and Warranties
Welcome! This lesson is about conditions and warranties.
A condition is essential: breach lets the buyer reject the goods. A warranty is collateral: breach gives damages only.
Implied conditions: title, description, fitness for a disclosed purpose, merchantable quality, and correspondence with sample.
Implied warranties: quiet possession, freedom from encumbrances, and disclosure of dangerous goods.
Caveat emptor means the buyer must take care, except where he relied on the seller's skill, bought by description or sample, or was defrauded.
The cafe told the seller it needed 300 cups a day and relied on his advice; the machine fails at 50. The fitness condition is broken, so the cafe may reject it. || The cafe told the seller it needed three hundred cups a day, and relied on his advice. The machine fails at fifty cups.
