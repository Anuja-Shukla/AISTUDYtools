# Lesson 1.5 — Permutations and Combinations
Welcome! This lesson is about counting quickly: permutations and combinations.
The multiplication principle: if one job can be done in m ways and the next in n ways, both can be done in m × n ways. 3 shirts and 4 trousers make 12 outfits. || The multiplication principle. If one job can be done in m ways, and the next in n ways, both together can be done in m times n ways. Three shirts and four trousers make twelve outfits.
The addition principle: if a job can be done in m ways or in n other ways, there are m + n ways in all. 3 buses or 2 trains give 5 ways to travel. || The addition principle. If a job can be done in m ways, or in n other ways, there are m plus n ways in all. Three buses or two trains give five ways to travel.
n factorial means n × (n − 1) × … × 1. So 5! = 120, and by definition 0! = 1. || n factorial means n times n minus one, all the way down to one. So five factorial is a hundred and twenty. And by definition, zero factorial is one.
A permutation is an arrangement, where order matters. Gold, silver and bronze among 8 runners: 8 × 7 × 6 = 336. This is ⁸P₃ = 8! ÷ 5!. || A permutation is an arrangement, where order matters. Gold, silver and bronze among eight runners can go eight times seven times six ways, which is three hundred and thirty-six. This is eight P three, eight factorial over five factorial.
With repetition allowed, every place has all n choices. A 4-digit PIN using 0 to 9 has 10⁴ = 10,000 possibilities. || With repetition allowed, every place has all n choices. A four-digit PIN using zero to nine has ten to the power four, ten thousand, possibilities.
With identical items, divide by their factorials. ACCOUNT has 7 letters with C twice, so it has 7! ÷ 2! = 2,520 arrangements. || With identical items, divide by their factorials. The word ACCOUNT has seven letters, with C twice. So it has seven factorial over two factorial, two thousand five hundred and twenty, arrangements.
Round a table, fix one person and arrange the rest: (n − 1)!. Six people can sit in 5! = 120 ways. For a necklace, also divide by 2. || Around a table, fix one person and arrange the rest. That gives n minus one factorial. Six people can sit in five factorial, a hundred and twenty, ways. For a necklace, also divide by two, because it can be flipped over.
A combination is a selection, where order does not matter. Choosing 3 of 8 runners: ⁸C₃ = 336 ÷ 3! = 56. || A combination is a selection, where order does not matter. Choosing three of eight runners is eight C three, which is three hundred and thirty-six divided by three factorial, fifty-six.
Two useful facts: ⁿCᵣ = ⁿCₙ₋ᵣ, so ¹⁰C₈ = ¹⁰C₂ = 45. And choosing at least one of n things can be done in 2ⁿ − 1 ways. || Two useful facts. n C r equals n C n minus r, so ten C eight is the same as ten C two, forty-five. And choosing at least one of n things can be done in two to the n, minus one, ways.
A committee of 3 is chosen from 5 men and 4 women, with at least one woman. Total: ⁹C₃ = 84. All men: ⁵C₃ = 10. So 84 − 10 = 74. || A committee of three is chosen from five men and four women, with at least one woman. In total, nine C three is eighty-four. Committees of all men: five C three is ten. So eighty-four minus ten gives seventy-four.
Before you calculate, ask one question: does order matter? Arranging means permutation. Choosing means combination. || Before you calculate, ask one question. Does order matter? Arranging means permutation. Choosing means combination.
