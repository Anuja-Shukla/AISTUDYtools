# Lesson 3.6 — Correlation and Regression
Welcome! This lesson is about two related ideas: correlation, which measures how closely two variables move together, and regression, which estimates one variable from the other.
Start with a scatter diagram. Points rising from left to right show positive correlation. Points falling show negative correlation. A shapeless cloud shows little or none.
Kiran Traders' advertising spend, in ₹ thousand, and sales, in ₹ lakh, over five months: x = 2, 3, 5, 6, 9 and y = 5, 7, 8, 10, 15. The points clearly rise together. || Here is Kiran Traders' advertising spend, in thousands of rupees, and its sales, in lakhs, over five months. x is two, three, five, six and nine. y is five, seven, eight, ten and fifteen. The points clearly rise together.
Karl Pearson's coefficient: r = Σdxdy ÷ √(Σdx² × Σdy²), using deviations from the means, 5 and 9. Here Σdxdy = 41, Σdx² = 30 and Σdy² = 58. || Karl Pearson's coefficient, r, is the sum of d x d y, over the square root of the sum of d x squared times the sum of d y squared. The deviations are taken from the means, five and nine. Here the sum of d x d y is forty-one, the sum of d x squared is thirty, and the sum of d y squared is fifty-eight.
So r = 41 ÷ √1,740 ≈ 0.983: a very strong positive correlation. || So r is forty-one over the square root of one thousand seven hundred and forty, about zero point nine eight three. A very strong positive correlation.
r always lies between −1 and +1. r² ≈ 0.966, so about 97% of the variation in sales moves with advertising. But correlation alone does not prove that one causes the other. || r always lies between minus one and plus one. r squared is about zero point nine six six, so about ninety-seven percent of the variation in sales moves with advertising. For ranks, use Spearman's coefficient: R = 1 − 6Σd² ÷ [n(n² − 1)]. Two judges rank 5 trainees with Σd² = 4, so R = 1 − 24 ÷ 120 = 0.8. || For ranks, use Spearman's coefficient. R is one, minus six times the sum of d squared, over n times n squared minus one. Two judges rank five trainees, and the sum of d squared is four. So R is one minus twenty-four over a hundred and twenty, which is zero point eight.
Regression draws the best line through the points. The line of y on x is y − 9 = bᵧₓ(x − 5), using the means 9 and 5, with bᵧₓ = Σdxdy ÷ Σdx² = 41 ÷ 30 ≈ 1.367. || Regression draws the best line through the points. The line of y on x is y minus nine equals b y x, times x minus five, using the two means. Here b y x is the sum of d x d y over the sum of d x squared: forty-one over thirty, about one point three six seven.
To estimate sales for an advertising spend of ₹7 thousand: y = 9 + 1.367 × (7 − 5) ≈ 11.73, about ₹11.7 lakh. || To estimate sales for an advertising spend of seven thousand rupees: y is nine, plus one point three six seven times seven minus five. That is about eleven point seven three, or about eleven point seven lakh rupees.
The line of x on y uses bₓᵧ = Σdxdy ÷ Σdy² = 41 ÷ 58 ≈ 0.707. Both regression lines pass through the point of means, (5, 9). || The line of x on y uses b x y, the sum of d x d y over the sum of d y squared. The two coefficients are linked: r = ±√(bᵧₓ × bₓᵧ) = √(1.367 × 0.707) ≈ 0.983, with the same sign as the coefficients.
