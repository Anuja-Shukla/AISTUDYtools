# Business Economics Lesson 5.2 — The Instruments of Trade Policy
Welcome! This lesson is about the instruments of trade policy.
Countries protect industries for reasons such as infant industries, security, jobs and stopping dumping, but consumers pay more.
A tariff can be specific, ad valorem, or a compound of both.
A tariff raises the price, increases domestic output, cuts imports and earns revenue, with a deadweight loss.
With a 20 percent tariff on bicycles and 10 percent on parts, value added rises from 4,000 to 5,400: effective protection is 35 percent. || With a twenty percent tariff on bicycles and ten percent on parts, domestic value added rises from four thousand to five thousand four hundred rupees. So the effective rate of protection is thirty-five percent, far above the nominal twenty.
Non-tariff measures include quotas, voluntary export restraints, anti-dumping duties and technical standards.
