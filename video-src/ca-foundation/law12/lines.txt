# Business Laws Lesson 1.2 — Nature of Contracts
Welcome! This lesson is about the nature of contracts: offer, acceptance, the essentials of a valid contract, and how contracts are classified.
Under the Indian Contract Act, a proposal accepted becomes a promise, a promise is an agreement, and an agreement enforceable by law is a contract.
So every contract is an agreement, but not every agreement is a contract. A promise to meet a friend for dinner is an agreement, but it is not enforceable.
Section 10 lists the essentials: free consent, competent parties, lawful consideration, a lawful object, and the agreement must not be expressly declared void. || Section ten lists the essentials. Free consent, competent parties, lawful consideration, a lawful object, and the agreement must not be expressly declared void.
Not every display is an offer. A price list or goods in a shop window are invitations to offer. A reward notice to the public is a general offer that anyone can accept by acting.
Acceptance must be absolute. If you reply 'I accept, but at ₹500 less', that is a counter-offer, and it rejects the original offer. || Acceptance must be absolute. If you reply, I accept, but at five hundred rupees less, that is a counter-offer, and it rejects the original offer.
Now the postal rule. Ravi posts an offer on 1 June. Neha posts her acceptance on 4 June, and it reaches Ravi on 7 June. On 5 June Ravi tries to revoke. || Now the postal rule. Ravi posts an offer on the first of June. Neha posts her acceptance on the fourth, and it reaches Ravi on the seventh. On the fifth, Ravi tries to revoke his offer.
The acceptance is complete against Ravi when it is posted, on 4 June. His revocation on 5 June is too late. A binding contract exists. || The acceptance is complete against Ravi when it is posted, on the fourth of June. So his revocation on the fifth is too late, and a binding contract exists.
Contracts are classified by validity: valid; void, with no legal effect; voidable, at one party's option; illegal; and unenforceable because of a technical defect. || Contracts are classified by validity. Valid. Void, with no legal effect. Voidable, at the option of one party. Illegal. And unenforceable, because of a technical defect such as missing stamp duty.
Try this: a contract made under coercion is… The answer is voidable at the option of the party coerced, under section 19. || Try this. A contract made under coercion is what? The answer is A. It is voidable, at the option of the party who was coerced, under section nineteen.
