# Business Economics Lesson 4.5 — The Demand for Money: Important Theories
Welcome! This lesson is about the demand for money and its important theories.
Money's primary functions are exchange and measuring value; it also stores value and settles future payments.
Fisher's equation is MV equals PT. Here prices are 2, and if money doubles with V and T fixed, prices double to 4. || Fisher's equation is M V equals P T. With money of five thousand crore, velocity four, and transactions of ten thousand, the price level is two. If money doubles, with velocity and transactions fixed, prices double to four.
The Cambridge version says people hold a fraction k of money income: k is one over V, so 0.25 here. || The Cambridge version says people hold a fraction k of money income as cash. K is one over velocity, so zero point two five here, and money demand equals five thousand crore.
Keynes added three motives: transactions and precautionary depend on income; speculative depends inversely on the interest rate.
At very low interest rates, money demand becomes infinite: the liquidity trap. Baumol, Tobin and Friedman refined the theory.
