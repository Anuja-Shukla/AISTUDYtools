# Accounting Lesson 4.7 — Issue of Shares
Welcome! This lesson is about the issue of shares.
Share capital comes in layers: authorised, issued, subscribed, called up and paid up.
Sunrise Textiles has 10 lakh authorised capital. Of 60,000 shares issued, 8 rupees are called; with 4,000 in arrears, paid-up capital is 4,76,000. || Sunrise Textiles has ten lakh of authorised capital. Of sixty thousand shares issued, eight rupees per share are called. With four thousand in arrears, paid-up capital is four lakh seventy six thousand.
Money comes in stages: application, allotment and calls, each recorded first as due and then as received.
A premium goes to securities premium, whose uses are limited by section 52. Shares cannot be issued at a discount, except sweat equity.
When a public issue is over-subscribed, some applications are rejected and others get a pro-rata allotment, with excess money adjusted against allotment.
Try this: calls in arrears are deducted from called-up capital. || Try this. How are calls in arrears shown? The answer is A, deducted from called-up capital.
