# Accounting Lesson 2.3 — Trial Balance
Welcome! This lesson is about the trial balance.
A trial balance lists all ledger balances on one date in debit and credit columns. If the books are arithmetically correct, the totals agree.
Meera's balances total 2,68,000 on each side, so the trial balance agrees. || Meera's balances total two lakh sixty eight thousand on each side, so the trial balance agrees.
A trial balance can agree even with errors: a transaction omitted altogether, errors that cancel each other, errors of principle, or posting to the wrong account of the same side.
It reveals one-sided postings, wrong totals, wrong amounts on one side, and balances placed on the wrong side.
If the totals differ, the difference is put in a suspense account temporarily, and cleared as the errors are located. || If the totals differ, the difference goes to a suspense account for the time being, and is cleared as the errors are found.
Try this: a completely omitted transaction does not upset the trial balance. || Try this. Which error leaves the trial balance agreeing? The answer is A, a transaction completely omitted.
