# Business Economics Lesson 5.4 — Exchange Rate and its Economic Effects
Welcome! This lesson is about the exchange rate and its economic effects.
India uses direct quotes: rupees per dollar. An indirect quote gives dollars per rupee.
The rate is set by demand for and supply of foreign exchange. India runs a managed float.
When the rate moves from 80 to 84 rupees, the dollar rises 5 percent but the rupee falls about 4.76 percent. || When the rate moves from eighty to eighty-four rupees per dollar, the dollar rises five percent, but the rupee falls only about four point seven six percent. The euro cross rate is ninety-two rupees forty.
A market-driven fall is depreciation; an official cut under a fixed rate is devaluation.
Depreciation helps exporters, makes imports dearer, can raise inflation, and improves the trade balance only after a lag.
