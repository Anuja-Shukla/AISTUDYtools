# Lesson 3.3 — Measures of Dispersion
Welcome! An average tells you the centre of the data. Dispersion tells you how spread out it is. In this lesson: range, quartile deviation, mean deviation, standard deviation and the coefficient of variation.
Here are weekly sales, in ₹ thousand, of two sales staff at Kiran Traders. A: 10, 12, 14, 16, 18. B: 5, 10, 20, 25, 30. Their averages are close, but B's sales jump around far more. || Here are weekly sales, in thousands of rupees, of two sales staff at Kiran Traders. A sold ten, twelve, fourteen, sixteen and eighteen. B sold five, ten, twenty, twenty-five and thirty. Their averages are close, but B's sales jump around far more.
Absolute measures, like the range or standard deviation, are in the data's own units. Relative measures, or coefficients, have no units, so you can compare different data sets.
The range is largest minus smallest. For A it is 18 − 10 = 8. The coefficient of range is (L − S) ÷ (L + S) = 8 ÷ 28, about 0.29. || The range is the largest value minus the smallest. For A, it is eighteen minus ten, which is eight. The coefficient of range is L minus S, over L plus S. That is eight over twenty-eight, about zero point two nine.
The quartile deviation is half the gap between the quartiles: (Q₃ − Q₁) ÷ 2. It measures the spread of the middle 50% and ignores extreme values. || The quartile deviation is half the gap between the quartiles. Q three minus Q one, over two. It measures the spread of the middle fifty percent, and ignores extreme values.
The mean deviation is the average distance from the centre. For A, the distances from the mean of 14 are 4, 2, 0, 2 and 4, so MD = 12 ÷ 5 = 2.4. It is smallest when taken about the median. || The mean deviation is the average distance from the centre. For A, the distances from the mean of fourteen are four, two, zero, two and four. So the mean deviation is twelve over five, which is two point four. It is smallest when taken about the median.
The standard deviation squares the deviations instead. For A: 16 + 4 + 0 + 4 + 16 = 40. Divide by 5 to get the variance, 8, then take the square root: σ ≈ 2.83. || The standard deviation squares the deviations instead. For A, sixteen plus four plus zero plus four plus sixteen is forty. Divide by five to get the variance, eight. Then take the square root. Sigma is about two point eight three.
Do the same for B. The squared deviations add up to 430, the variance is 86, and σ ≈ 9.27. || Do the same for B. The squared deviations add up to four hundred and thirty. The variance is eighty-six, and sigma is about nine point two seven.
To compare consistency, use the coefficient of variation: CV = σ ÷ mean × 100. A: 2.83 ÷ 14 ≈ 20.2%. B: 9.27 ÷ 18 ≈ 51.5%. Lower CV means more consistent, so A is more consistent. || To compare consistency, use the coefficient of variation. C V is sigma over the mean, times a hundred. For A, that's about twenty point two percent. For B, about fifty-one point five percent. A lower C V means more consistent, so A is more consistent.
Two rules worth remembering. Adding a constant to every value does not change σ. Multiplying every value by k multiplies σ by k. || Two rules worth remembering. Adding a constant to every value does not change sigma. Multiplying every value by k multiplies sigma by k.
A handy result: the SD of the first n natural numbers is √[(n² − 1) ÷ 12]. For 1 to 5, that is √2 ≈ 1.414. || A handy result. The standard deviation of the first n natural numbers is the square root of n squared minus one, over twelve. For one to five, that is root two, about one point four one four.
Try this: the SD of a data set is 4. Each value is multiplied by 3, and then 5 is added. The new SD is 3 × 4 = 12. Adding 5 changes nothing. || Try this. The standard deviation of a data set is four. Each value is multiplied by three, and then five is added. What is the new standard deviation? Three times four is twelve. Adding five changes nothing. The answer is A.
