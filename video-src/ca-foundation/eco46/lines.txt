# Business Economics Lesson 4.6 — The Concept of Money Supply
Welcome! This lesson is about the concept of money supply.
The RBI measures money from M0 to M4. Narrow money is M1; broad money, M3, adds time deposits. || The RBI measures money from M zero to M four. Narrow money is M one. Broad money, M three, adds time deposits with banks.
Banks keep only a fraction of each deposit as reserves and lend the rest, which is redeposited and lent again.
With c of 0.2 and r of 0.1, the multiplier is 4, so 2,000 crore of high-powered money supports 8,000 crore. || With a currency-deposit ratio of zero point two, and a reserve ratio of zero point one, the multiplier is four. So two thousand crore of high-powered money supports eight thousand crore of money supply.
If the reserve ratio falls to 0.05, the multiplier rises to 4.8 and money supply to 9,600 crore. || If the reserve ratio falls to zero point zero five, the multiplier rises to four point eight, and money supply to nine thousand six hundred crore.
Money supply depends on the monetary base, reserve requirements, the public's preference for currency, and the demand for credit.
