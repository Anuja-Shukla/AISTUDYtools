# Lesson 1.2 — Equations
Welcome! This lesson is about equations: linear, simultaneous and quadratic, with a quick look at cubics.
An equation says two expressions are equal. Solving it means finding the value that makes it true. For example, 2x + 5 = 17 gives x = 6. || An equation says two expressions are equal. Solving it means finding the value that makes it true. For example, two x plus five equals seventeen gives x equals six.
Kiran Traders sells chairs at ₹50 each. Each chair costs ₹30 to make, and fixed costs are ₹10,000 a month. How many chairs must it sell to break even? || Kiran Traders sells chairs at fifty rupees each. Each chair costs thirty rupees to make, and fixed costs are ten thousand rupees a month. How many chairs must it sell to break even?
Break-even means revenue equals cost: 50x = 30x + 10,000. So 20x = 10,000, and x = 500 chairs. || Break-even means revenue equals cost. Fifty x equals thirty x plus ten thousand. So twenty x equals ten thousand, and x is five hundred chairs.
Two unknowns need two equations. Take 2x + 3y = 13 and 3x − y = 3. || Two unknowns need two equations. Take two x plus three y equals thirteen, and three x minus y equals three.
From the second, y = 3x − 3. Substitute it into the first: 2x + 9x − 9 = 13, so 11x = 22 and x = 2. Then y = 3. || From the second equation, y equals three x minus three. Substitute it into the first. Two x plus nine x minus nine equals thirteen. So eleven x equals twenty-two, and x is two. Then y is three.
Always check in both equations. Elimination or Cramer's rule would give the same answer. || Always check your answer in both equations. Elimination, or Cramer's rule, would give exactly the same answer.
A quadratic has the form ax² + bx + c = 0, where a is not zero. One formula solves every quadratic. || A quadratic has the form a x squared plus b x plus c equals zero, where a is not zero. One formula solves every quadratic.
For x² − 5x + 6 = 0: D = b² − 4ac = 25 − 24 = 1. So x = (5 ± 1) ÷ 2, which gives 3 or 2. || For x squared minus five x plus six equals zero, the discriminant D is b squared minus four a c, which is twenty-five minus twenty-four, or one. So x is five plus or minus one, over two. That gives three or two.
The discriminant tells you the nature of the roots. D > 0: two real, unequal roots. D = 0: two equal roots. D < 0: no real roots. || The discriminant tells you the nature of the roots. If D is positive, there are two real, unequal roots. If D is zero, the two roots are equal. If D is negative, there are no real roots.
Two shortcuts: the sum of the roots is −b/a and the product is c/a. Here 3 + 2 = 5 and 3 × 2 = 6. So an equation with given roots is x² − (sum)x + product = 0. || Two shortcuts. The sum of the roots is minus b over a, and the product is c over a. Here, three plus two is five, and three times two is six. So an equation with given roots is x squared, minus the sum times x, plus the product, equals zero.
For a cubic, find one root by trial, then divide it out. x³ − 6x² + 11x − 6 = 0 has root x = 1, which leaves x² − 5x + 6. So the roots are 1, 2 and 3. || For a cubic, find one root by trial, then divide it out. x cubed minus six x squared plus eleven x minus six has the root x equals one. That leaves x squared minus five x plus six. So the roots are one, two and three.
Try this: one root of x² − kx + 8 = 0 is 2. What is k? The product of the roots is 8, so the other root is 4. Their sum, 6, is k. || Try this. One root of x squared minus k x plus eight equals zero is two. What is k? Think for a moment. The product of the roots is eight, so the other root is four. Their sum, six, is k. The answer is A.
