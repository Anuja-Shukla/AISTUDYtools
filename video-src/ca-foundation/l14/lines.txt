# Lesson 1.4 — Mathematics of Finance
Welcome! This lesson covers the mathematics of finance: interest, annuities, loans and investment decisions.
Simple interest is earned only on the original amount. Compound interest also earns interest on the interest already added.
Take ₹10,000 at 10% a year for 3 years. Simple interest gives ₹13,000. Compound interest gives ₹13,310. || Take ten thousand rupees at ten percent a year, for three years. Simple interest gives thirteen thousand rupees. Compound interest gives thirteen thousand three hundred and ten.
When interest is added more often, use the rate per period. For R% a year compounded k times a year, i = R ÷ 100k and n = k × years. || When interest is added more often, use the rate per period. For R percent a year, compounded k times a year, i is R over a hundred k, and n is k times the number of years.
Riya deposits ₹50,000 at 8% a year, compounded quarterly, for 2 years. So i = 2% and n = 8, and A = 50,000 × 1.02⁸ ≈ ₹58,583. || Riya deposits fifty thousand rupees at eight percent a year, compounded quarterly, for two years. So i is two percent, and n is eight. The amount is fifty thousand times one point zero two to the power eight, about fifty-eight thousand five hundred and eighty-three rupees.
The effective rate is the yearly rate that gives the same result: E = (1 + i)ᵏ − 1. Here 1.02⁴ − 1 ≈ 8.24%. And 12% compounded monthly is about 12.68%. || The effective rate is the yearly rate that gives the same result. E equals one plus i, to the power k, minus one. Here that is about eight point two four percent. And twelve percent compounded monthly is about twelve point six eight percent.
Reducing-balance depreciation works in reverse: value = P(1 − r)ⁿ. A ₹2,00,000 machine at 10% a year is worth ₹1,45,800 after 3 years. || Reducing balance depreciation works in reverse. The value is P times one minus r, to the power n. A two lakh rupee machine, at ten percent a year, is worth one lakh forty-five thousand eight hundred after three years.
An annuity is a series of equal payments at equal intervals, like a monthly SIP, a lease, or a loan EMI.
For payments at the end of each period, FV = A[(1 + i)ⁿ − 1] ÷ i and PV = A[1 − (1 + i)⁻ⁿ] ÷ i. For an annuity due, paid at the start, multiply by (1 + i). || For payments at the end of each period, the future value is A times one plus i to the n, minus one, all over i. The present value is A times one minus one plus i to the minus n, over i. For an annuity due, paid at the start of each period, multiply by one plus i.
A perpetuity pays forever, so its present value is simply A ÷ i. ₹6,000 a year at 12% is worth ₹50,000 today. || A perpetuity pays forever, so its present value is simply A divided by i. Six thousand rupees a year, at twelve percent, is worth fifty thousand rupees today.
A loan repaid in equal instalments is an annuity too. EMI = P × i(1 + i)ⁿ ÷ [(1 + i)ⁿ − 1]. A ₹5 lakh loan at 9% for 5 years has an EMI of about ₹10,379. || A loan repaid in equal instalments is an annuity too. The EMI is P times i, times one plus i to the n, divided by one plus i to the n, minus one. A five lakh rupee loan at nine percent, for five years, has an EMI of about ten thousand three hundred and seventy-nine rupees.
To judge a project, NPV = present value of inflows − present value of outflows. A ₹1 lakh project returning ₹30k, ₹40k, ₹50k and ₹20k has an NPV of about ₹11,557 at 10%. Positive, so accept. || To judge a project, the net present value is the present value of inflows, minus the present value of outflows. A one lakh rupee project returning thirty, forty, fifty and twenty thousand has an NPV of about eleven thousand five hundred and fifty-seven rupees, at ten percent. It is positive, so accept.
CAGR is the steady yearly growth rate: (end ÷ start)^(1/n) − 1. ₹1,00,000 growing to ₹1,61,051 in 5 years is a CAGR of exactly 10%. || CAGR is the steady yearly growth rate. Divide the end value by the start value, take the n-th root, and subtract one. One lakh growing to one lakh sixty-one thousand and fifty-one in five years is a CAGR of exactly ten percent.
