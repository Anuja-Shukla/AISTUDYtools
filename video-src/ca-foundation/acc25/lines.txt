# Accounting Lesson 2.5 — Cash Book
Welcome! This lesson is about the cash book.
A cash book may be simple, with cash and bank columns, with a discount column too, or a petty cash book for small expenses.
Cash discount allowed to customers is on the debit side; discount received from suppliers is on the credit side. Trade discount is never recorded.
When cash moves between the office and the bank, both sides are in the cash book. These contra entries are marked C and not posted to the ledger.
Starting with 20,000 cash and 40,000 in the bank, after the week's entries Meera has 4,000 cash and 60,200 in the bank. || Starting with twenty thousand in cash and forty thousand in the bank, after the week's entries Meera has four thousand in cash and sixty thousand two hundred in the bank.
The cash column can never show a credit balance; a credit in the bank column is an overdraft; and discount columns are only totalled.
Try this: a credit balance in the cash column means there is an error. || Try this. What does a credit balance in the cash column show? The answer is A, an error, because you cannot pay out more cash than you have.
