# Lesson 1.6 — Sequences and Series: AP and GP
Welcome! This lesson covers sequences and series: arithmetic and geometric progressions, means, and standard sums.
A sequence is an ordered list of numbers, and a series is their sum. Two patterns appear again and again: adding the same amount, and multiplying by the same ratio.
In an arithmetic progression, or AP, each term adds a fixed common difference, d. 4, 7, 10, 13 has d = 3. || In an arithmetic progression, or A P, each term adds a fixed common difference, d. Four, seven, ten, thirteen has a common difference of three.
The nth term is tₙ = a + (n − 1)d, and the sum of n terms is Sₙ = n/2 × [2a + (n − 1)d]. || The nth term is a, plus n minus one times d. And the sum of n terms is n over two, times two a plus n minus one times d.
Riya saves ₹500 in month 1 and ₹100 more each month. In month 12 she saves 500 + 11 × 100 = ₹1,600. In the year she saves 6 × (1,000 + 1,100) = ₹12,600. || Riya saves five hundred rupees in month one, and a hundred rupees more each month. In month twelve she saves five hundred plus eleven hundreds, sixteen hundred rupees. In the whole year she saves six times twenty-one hundred, which is twelve thousand six hundred rupees.
In a geometric progression, or GP, each term is multiplied by a fixed common ratio, r. 3, 6, 12, 24 has r = 2. || In a geometric progression, or G P, each term is multiplied by a fixed common ratio, r. Three, six, twelve, twenty-four has a common ratio of two.
The nth term is arⁿ⁻¹ and the sum is a(rⁿ − 1)/(r − 1). So the 8th term of 3, 6, 12, … is 3 × 2⁷ = 384, and the sum of 8 terms is 3(2⁸ − 1) = 765. || The nth term is a times r to the n minus one. The sum is a times r to the n minus one, over r minus one. So the eighth term of three, six, twelve is three times two to the seven, three hundred and eighty-four. And the sum of eight terms is seven hundred and sixty-five.
When r is between −1 and 1 the terms shrink and the sum settles: S∞ = a/(1 − r). So 1 + 1/2 + 1/4 + … = 2. || When r is between minus one and one, the terms shrink, and the sum settles down. The sum to infinity is a over one minus r. So one plus a half plus a quarter, and so on, adds up to exactly two.
For two numbers, the arithmetic mean is (a + b)/2 and the geometric mean is √(ab). For 4 and 16: AM = 10, GM = 8. For positive numbers, AM ≥ GM. || For two numbers, the arithmetic mean is a plus b over two, and the geometric mean is the square root of a b. For four and sixteen, the A M is ten and the G M is eight. For positive numbers, the A M is always at least the G M.
Three standard sums: Σn = n(n + 1)/2, Σn² = n(n + 1)(2n + 1)/6 and Σn³ = [n(n + 1)/2]². So 1 + 2 + … + 100 = 5,050. || Three standard sums. The sum of the first n numbers is n times n plus one, over two. The sum of squares is n, n plus one, two n plus one, over six. And the sum of cubes is the square of the first sum. So one plus two, all the way to a hundred, is five thousand and fifty.
